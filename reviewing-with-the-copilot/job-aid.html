The four checks

1
Evidence. What supports the AI suggestion? Open every cited timestamp. Does the moment support both the descriptor and its level? Remove or lower anything unsupported; a descriptor with no matching moment stays unsupported until you find one.
2
Context. How does presentation change interpretation? Tone, genre, realism, injury detail, and how long the camera stays. The TV guidelines name the levels; place a scene with your team's approved classification.
3
Count. What do frequency, duration, or persistence change? Look at moments together. A cluster can be one sustained sequence. Some MPA rules turn on how many times, or in what context, something appears.
4
System and policy. Which approved rating system does the title's metadata name? Is the version the one that was rated? Does an additional review rule, such as a market-specific policy, apply?

TV Parental Guidelines: levels

TV-Y7Children's programs: mild fantasy or comedic violence. FV: fantasy violence more intense or combative than other TV-Y7 programs
TV-GLittle or no violence, no strong language, little or no sexual dialogue or situations
TV-PGModerate violence (V), some sexual situations (S), infrequent coarse language (L), some suggestive dialogue (D)
TV-14Intense V, intense S, strong coarse L, intensely suggestive D
TV-MAGraphic V, explicit sexual activity, crude indecent L

The guidelines name levels; they set no fixed rules about blood or camera time.

MPA rules that turn on count or context

  • One harsher sexually derived word, only as an expletive: initially at least PG-13.
  • More than one, or even one in a sexual context: ordinarily R. Exception: the Rating Board may rate it PG-13 by a two-thirds special vote (context, manner, or inconspicuous use). An analyst estimate cannot apply it.
  • Any drug use: initially at least PG-13. A PG film has no drug use content.
  • Violence: PG-13 generally excludes violence that is both realistic and extreme or persistent. No numeric threshold; the Board weighs it.
  • Nudity: PG may include brief nudity; more than brief requires at least PG-13, generally not sexually oriented.
  • Only the MPA Rating Board issues MPA ratings. An unedited film with an MPA rating displays it; an edited version needs its own rating.

Rationale format

Claim: action, final rating, descriptor.
Evidence: the moments, with timestamps.
Policy: the guideline or rule that connects them.

Example

Override to TV-14, Violence (V). · Assault 00:37:10; injury shown 00:40:05, in a sustained break-in. · TV-PG covers moderate violence; TV-14 may contain intense violence.

Escalation checklist

  • Reason: why the rating rules alone can't settle it
  • Source evidence: timestamps and what each shows
  • Unresolved question: the decision Policy needs to make
  • Policy reference: the guideline or review rule that applies
  • Publication hold: in place where a review rule requires it, and noted in the record

Example review rule (fictional): Training Market X refers approving depictions of a designated restricted symbol to Policy before publication.

What is real here: rating definitions paraphrase the published U.S. TV Parental Guidelines and the MPA Classification and Rating Rules (effective as revised July 24, 2020). The four-action workflow, QA sampling, and Training Market X are illustrative for a training simulation and do not represent Amazon's proprietary tools or policies, or any real country's rules.
