The problem

The Sanger Learning Center wanted to bring back the Student Educator Forum as a central place to train and professionalize the hundreds of student employees who support learning across UT Austin. The forum had not run since February 2022, and nobody had a current plan to work from.

The date was set for mid-April, close to finals and end-of-year fatigue. The internal target was modest: about 40 attendees, mostly the center's own student educators, plus invited campus partners. The bar was to show the event could run again before scaling it.

A committee of seven split the work across four functions. My co-lead and I owned the connecting pieces: the Zoom environment, registration, the reminder schedule, breakout-room assignments, and the run of show on the day.

Decisions

  1. Run it as a small soft launch

    Design challenge
    The event was being rebuilt without a playbook, at an awkward point in the semester.
    Decision
    Keep the first cycle small and virtual, with a target of about 40, and document everything so the next cycle could reuse it.
    Rationale
    Proving the operations worked mattered more than size in year one, and a documented template lowers the cost of running it again.
    Evidence
    Attendance reached 70, and the debrief reported no operational incidents on the day.

  2. Let attendees choose sessions in a round-robin format

    Design challenge
    Nine student presentations had to fit into a three-hour afternoon without losing people between sessions.
    Decision
    Group the presentations into three themed tracks (professional and interpersonal skills, learning and facilitation, well-being and resilience) and let attendees pick a breakout at each round.
    Rationale
    Choice keeps attendees in the room for topics that matter to them, and themed tracks make the choice quick.
    Limitation
    The survey showed that side conversations and connection were harder to create in virtual breakouts.

  3. Give every moderator and presenter the same written guidance

    Design challenge
    Six moderators and many first-time presenters needed to handle timing, chat, slides, and no-shows consistently.
    Decision
    Write one moderator guide and one templated presenter email, plus Zoom etiquette guidelines for everyone.
    Rationale
    A single source of truth reduces day-of questions and makes the event repeatable with new volunteers.

What the survey and debrief said

  • Move it earlier. The April date was the most common complaint; February or early March came up repeatedly.
  • Virtual breakouts limit connection. The side conversations that make peer-educator events valuable were harder online.
  • Give presenters something portable. Presenters wanted a certificate or credential they could share.
  • Widen the audience. Recruitment ran through program directors; graduate students and student organizations asked to be included.

Next cycle

The center now has a documented template for running the forum again. From the survey and debrief, the changes I would make:

  • Move the date to February or early March.
  • Add presenter coaching: one 30-minute slot per presenter in late March. Several were giving their first conference talk, and presence is harder to project on video.
  • Issue a digital certificate for presenters, which also gives next year's recruitment something concrete to offer.
  • Recruit through student organizations and graduate communities as well as program directors.
