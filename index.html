About Me

I design engaging learning experiences that help people develop skills they can use confidently long after training ends. My approach begins with understanding the learner: what they already know, where they may struggle, and what will help them stay engaged as they build proficiency.

My background spans instructional design, curriculum development, learning operations, and program management. At UT Austin’s Sanger Learning Center, I support a peer educator program serving approximately 2,500 students each semester, developing training, improving learning experiences, and managing the systems that support our student educators.

What Guides My Design.

I believe learning should be engaging, purposeful, and built to last. My goal isn’t simply to help someone complete a course or understand something in the moment. I want them to leave with knowledge and skills they can retrieve, apply, and continue developing.

That means paying attention to how people learn, where they get stuck, and what kinds of practice help them move from understanding a concept to using it independently.

  1. 01

    Engagement With Purpose

    I want learners to actively work with the material, not just move through it. Whether I’m developing an interactive module, facilitating a workshop, or building a course, I look for opportunities that encourage curiosity, decision-making, and meaningful participation.

    Engagement should serve the learning, not distract from it.

  2. 02

    Learning That Lasts

    Understanding something once isn’t the same as being able to use it later. I incorporate opportunities for retrieval, application, reflection, and repeated practice so learners can build confidence and proficiency over time.

    My goal is to help learners become increasingly independent, rather than dependent on the training itself.

  3. 03

    Removing Barriers to Learning

    I pay attention to where learners lose momentum, become confused, or struggle to apply what they’ve learned. Those moments provide valuable information about what needs to change.

    Sometimes the solution is clearer instruction. Other times, it’s a different activity, additional scaffolding, a more accessible format, or an opportunity to practice in a new context.

From Concept to Learning Experience.

Every project starts with a different challenge, but my process follows a consistent set of questions: Who am I designing for? What do they need to learn or accomplish? What might get in their way? And how will I know whether the experience is working?

I use those questions to guide my decisions from the initial concept through development, implementation, and revision.

  1. 01

    Understand the Learner

    I begin by identifying the audience, their existing knowledge, and the skills they need to develop.

    Details & key activities

    Depending on the project, that might involve reviewing performance data, speaking with stakeholders, gathering learner feedback, or examining existing materials.

    I also look for practical barriers, including time, accessibility, unclear expectations, and gaps in foundational knowledge.

    Key activitiesLearner analysis, stakeholder conversations, needs assessment, performance-gap identification, and review of existing materials.

  2. 02

    Establish Learning Goals

    Once I understand the audience and the challenge, I identify what learners should be able to do by the end of the experience.

    Details & key activities

    I distinguish between information learners need to understand and skills they need to demonstrate. From there, I align objectives, instructional activities, and assessments.

    Key activitiesLearning objectives, task analysis, curriculum mapping, content sequencing, and assessment planning.

  3. 03

    Design for Engagement & Practice

    This is where I consider how learners will interact with the material. I select instructional strategies based on what learners need to practice.

    Details & key activities

    That might involve scenarios, simulations, collaborative activities, guided reflection, interactive models, or opportunities to retrieve and apply information.

    I consider the amount of support learners need initially and how that support can be reduced as they become more proficient.

    Key activitiesStoryboarding, instructional strategy selection, scaffolding, authentic practice, interaction design, and formative assessment.

  4. 04

    Develop, Test & Refine

    I build and refine learning materials through an iterative process.

    Details & key activities

    I consider clarity, accessibility, navigation, cognitive load, and how effectively each activity supports its intended objective.

    I use development tools, including AI-assisted workflows where appropriate, to prototype ideas and accelerate production while retaining responsibility for instructional accuracy and quality.

    Key activitiesPrototyping, authoring, usability review, accessibility checks, quality assurance, and revision.

  5. 05

    Evaluate & Improve

    I look beyond whether learners enjoyed or completed the experience. I want to understand what they learned, where they continued to struggle, and whether they can apply their knowledge independently.

    Details & key activities

    Depending on the project, that might involve assessment results, learner feedback, observations, engagement data, or longer-term performance measures.

    Findings inform future revisions and help identify where additional support may be needed.

    Key activitiesFormative and summative evaluation, learner feedback, assessment analysis, performance review, and continuous improvement.

↺ These stages recur and overlap. Discovery, development, testing, and revision often happen together, and what I learn from evaluation feeds the next round of design.

Educator
Forum.

RoleOperations co-lead
ForSanger · UT Austin
StatusImplemented · April 2026

A relaunch of the Sanger Learning Center’s peer educator conference, inactive since 2022, as a central place to train and professionalize the hundreds of student employees who tutor, mentor, coach, and facilitate learning across UT Austin. Run as a deliberate soft launch to prove the event could work again before scaling it, the April 2026 forum drew 70 attendees against a target of 40 and an average satisfaction rating of 4.6 out of 5.

My roleAs logistics and operations co-lead on a seven-person committee, I shared ownership of the pieces that connected everything: the Zoom environment, registration, the reminder schedule, breakout-room assignments, and the day-of run of show. The work was rebuilt from a near-blank slate in twelve weeks, with a post-event survey and debrief that set the changes for the next cycle.
