About Me

I design, develop, and manage learning experiences and educational programs with an emphasis on instructional alignment, meaningful engagement, and long-term proficiency. My approach is grounded in learning science, with careful attention to how learning objectives, instructional strategies, and assessments work together to support measurable outcomes.

My work encompasses the full lifecycle of curriculum and e-learning initiatives, from initial needs analysis and conceptual development through instructional design, production, implementation, and evaluation. I manage the academic and operational dimensions of these projects, including stakeholder collaboration, resource planning, timelines, deliverables, and continuous improvement informed by learner feedback and performance data.

My experience spans K–12 education, higher education, and digital learning, including curriculum development, educator training, instructional technology, and the management of large-scale learning programs. Across these settings, I focus on creating effective learning experiences while establishing the processes and systems needed to implement and sustain them.

04How I think

What Guides My Work.

I design for the moment someone needs to use what they've learned, not just the moment they're introduced to it. Three priorities shape my decisions.

  1. 01

    Engagement With Purpose

    Learners should actively work with the material. I use discussion, problem-solving, and meaningful decisions to encourage participation that supports the learning objective.

  2. 02

    Learning That Lasts

    Understanding a concept once doesn't guarantee proficiency. I incorporate retrieval, feedback, and opportunities for repeated application so learners can use what they've learned independently.

  3. 03

    Removing Barriers to Learning

    When learners struggle, I look at why. Missing foundational knowledge, unclear expectations, accessibility challenges, and unnecessary complexity can all interfere with progress. Identifying those barriers helps me design more effective support.

05How I work

From Challenge to Learning Experience.

Before I build a module or choose a tool, I identify what learners need to do and what's getting in their way. That understanding shapes the learning goals, opportunities for practice, development, and evaluation.

Stage 01

Understand the Learner

What do learners already know, what are they expected to do, and where are they encountering difficulty?

What I do

Review performance data, gather feedback, consult stakeholders, and examine existing materials and learning conditions.

What this produces

A clearer definition of learner needs, constraints, and the performance gap the experience should address.

Stage 02

Define Learning Goals

What should learners be able to demonstrate by the end of the experience?

What I do

Translate identified needs into measurable objectives, prioritize essential content, and align activities and assessments with the intended outcomes.

What this produces

Learning objectives, content organization, curriculum maps, and assessment criteria.

Stage 03

Design the Practice

How can learners move from understanding an idea to using it with confidence?

What I do

Develop scenarios, collaborative activities, guided practice, reflection, retrieval opportunities, and feedback that supports growing independence.

What this produces

Storyboards, activity designs, facilitation plans, and opportunities to demonstrate learning.

Stage 04

Build, Test & Refine

Does the experience work as intended for the people using it?

What I do

Develop and prototype materials, review clarity and navigation, check accessibility and accuracy, and revise based on testing and feedback.

What this produces

Functional learning materials, documented review findings, and targeted improvements.

Stage 05

Evaluate & Improve

What can learners now demonstrate, and what still needs attention?

What I do

Review assessment results, learner feedback, engagement patterns, and performance evidence. Where feasible, examine whether skills transfer beyond the initial experience.

What this produces

Evaluation findings, recommendations, and priorities for future revisions.

Discovery, design, testing, and evaluation frequently overlap. Findings from one stage can send me back to another, and that is a normal part of developing an effective learning experience.
